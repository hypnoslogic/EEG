Curriculum map

This site is built on two frameworks: the learning outcomes agreed in the 2026 international Delphi consensus[3] and the course structure of the Safe Brain Initiative EEG boot camp[4].

The Delphi learning outcomes

Thirty-three international experts reached consensus on 58 of 191 proposed outcomes: 46 at the Basic level and 12 at the Advanced level. Basic outcomes cover what is needed to use EEG in routine anaesthesia. Advanced outcomes are for those who teach, do research or give advanced EEG-guided care[3]. The outcome statements below are reproduced verbatim from Berger-Estilita et al. with attribution. Outcomes marked "planned" are not yet covered.

The boot camp structure

The Safe Brain Initiative boot camp, now an ESAIC research group, teaches in blocks that build on each other and finishes with hands-on consolidation. It relies on clinical algorithms, quizzes for repetition and activation, and transferring knowledge from theatre to ICU[4]. This site follows the same sequence:

Boot camp blockCovered on this site
1. Rationale for EEG monitoringStart here, 1 Fundamentals (why monitor), 6 Evidence
2. Understanding the EEG and the monitor displays; a typical general anaesthetic1 Fundamentals, 2 Raw EEG, 4 Processed EEG
3. Variation by situation: drugs, artefacts, nociception3 Drugs, 2 Artefacts (ShARP), 5 Nociception
4. Variation by patient: geriatric and paediatric; transfer to ICU sedation5 The vulnerable brain. ICU section planned
5. Practical consolidation: electrodes and monitors, quiz, video cases2 Getting a good signal. Quizzes and video cases planned. Hands-on sessions are best run locally
6. Debriefing and further learningFurther learning, Contributing
