Bottom line (October 2026)

Where things stand
  • Delirium in older adults: the trials disagree. Several RCTs and meta-analyses, including one from 2025, favour EEG guidance{{cite:chan2013,radtke2013,evered2021,da2025}}. The two ENGAGES trials, which reduced EEG suppression time specifically, found no effect{{cite:engages2019,engagescanada2024}}.
  • Mortality: targeting BIS 50 rather than 35 did not change 1-year mortality in high-risk older patients. The trial authors concluded that a broad range of depth is safe when volatile anaesthesia is titrated with pEEG{{cite:balanced2019}}.
  • Children: a 2026 meta-analysis found substantially less emergence delirium with EEG guidance{{cite:haidar2026}}.
  • Awareness: the clearest role is TIVA with neuromuscular block, where there is no end-tidal check{{cite:nap5,lee2021}}.
  • Monitors: different commercial indices can give different recommendations for the same EEG{{cite:hight2023}}. Understanding the raw EEG is the safeguard.

Why the trials disagree

  • What was targeted. Some trials avoided deep anaesthesia generally (an index range or BIS 50 vs 35). The ENGAGES trials aimed specifically at shortening suppression. The JAMA editorial accompanying ENGAGES-Canada (Berger and Neuman) suggested that using the EEG mainly to cut suppression time is unlikely to prevent delirium on its own. It also noted that trials showing benefit tended to enrol younger patients with fewer comorbidities{{cite:engagescanada2024}}.
  • How much separation was achieved between arms, and whether the control arm was already "EEG-aware".
  • Who was studied. Cardiac and non-cardiac surgery, regional populations, baseline frailty.
  • Delirium has many causes. Pain, inflammation, sleep loss, drugs and illness all contribute. Anaesthetic depth is only one factor that clinicians can change{{cite:lee2021}}.
How to read this for practice

No trial suggests EEG guidance causes harm, and several suggest anaesthetic use falls. The case for using EEG to individualise anaesthesia, avoiding both awareness and needless depth, is stronger than the case that any single EEG target prevents delirium. That is why this site emphasises reading the EEG rather than chasing a number.

Awareness

  • B-Aware found fewer cases of awareness with BIS guidance in high-risk patients{{cite:baware2004}}. B-Unaware and BAG-RECALL found BIS protocols were no better than structured end-tidal gas alarms in patients on volatile agents{{cite:avidan2008,avidan2011}}.
  • NAP5: awareness was more common with TIVA and with TIVA plus neuromuscular block. Many cases were related to drug delivery and judged avoidable{{cite:nap5}}.
  • Rates of awareness depend heavily on how it is detected. Spontaneous reporting gave about 1 in 19,000 in NAP5, and structured interviews about 1 in 100{{cite:nap5,errando2008}}.

Monitor agreement

Given identical emergence-like EEG signals, five commercial monitors gave discordant clinical recommendations{{cite:hight2023}}. See Module 4.

Guidelines and consensus

SourceRelevant statement
ASER/POQI joint consensus, 2020{{cite:poqi2020}}End-tidal gas monitoring with alarms, or pEEG, to reduce awareness with recall. The evidence was judged insufficient to recommend pEEG specifically to reduce delirium or POCD in older high-risk patients, while noting that three of the four large RCTs then available had shown less delirium{{cite:lee2021}}

Awaiting review: South African and other guidelines

South African guidance (SASA Practice Guidelines 2022) and further international monitoring standards have been identified as candidates but are not yet included until they have been reviewed. See the roadmap.
