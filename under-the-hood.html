Under the hood

Module 7 · optional · Advanced

By the end of this page you should be able to

  • Explain how a spectrum is estimated from a short window and what limits its resolution. Delphi PR3 Delphi QT1
  • Recognise how sampling rate and analysis settings shape what a monitor can show. Delphi FD14
  • Reproduce or extend the figures on this site, and run spectral analysis on your own monitor's raw EEG. Delphi LL6

From samples to a spectrum: Welch's method

A monitor samples the EEG voltage many times a second. To estimate the spectrum of the last few seconds:

  1. Take a window of recent samples, for example 4 s.
  2. Cut it into overlapping segments, for example 2 s long with 50% overlap.
  3. Taper each segment with a window function (Hann) so the abrupt cut at its edges does not leak false power into other frequencies.
  4. Take the FFT of each segment and square the magnitude to get power.
  5. Average the segments. Averaging reduces noise in the estimate at the cost of using more data.

The DSA is simply this calculation repeated every second or so and stacked side by side. The companion script uses exactly this method, and so do this site's figures.

Window length versus frequency resolution

The frequency resolution of a spectrum is Δf = 1 / segment length. A 2 s segment resolves 0.5 Hz steps. A 0.5 s segment only resolves 2 Hz steps and cannot separate the slow (< 1 Hz) and delta bands. Longer segments resolve frequency better but blur changes over time, which is part of why indices lag[41].

Trade-off

Multitaper methods, as used by Purdon's group, get more out of a short window, giving good resolution with low variance[1,42]. For a bedside display, 2 s segments within a 4 s window, recalculated every second, is a reasonable compromise and is the default in the companion script.

Sampling rate and what you can see

A signal sampled at fs can only represent frequencies below fs/2 (the Nyquist frequency). Purdon's recordings were made at 250 Hz[1]. If a monitor exports its EEG waveform at about 100 Hz, which is plausible for some serial interfaces but should be checked for your device, then:

  • nothing above about 50 Hz can be represented, so the 50 Hz mains line cannot be seen, or removed with a notch filter, in the exported data;
  • the upper end of Entropy's RE range (up to 47 Hz) is close to the limit[38];
  • everything an anaesthetist needs for drug signatures (0.1–35 Hz) is comfortably within range.

The companion real-time script

eeg_rt_spectrum.py reads the raw EEG waveform from a GE/Datex-Ohmeda monitor with an Entropy module over the RS232 computer interface and displays, in real time[50]:

  • the raw trace (4–60 s), optionally band-pass filtered to show up to three rhythms;
  • a Welch spectrum with shaded bands and SEF95;
  • a 10-minute DSA, with a choice of colour maps including the classic "jet";
  • relative band power, and switchable band schemes (standard, Purdon, split α/β, Entropy SE/RE, or custom);
  • optional CSV logging of raw samples and band powers.

Run python eeg_rt_spectrum.py --simulate to try it without a monitor. Download the script.

Research and education only

The script is not a medical device and must not guide clinical care. Its protocol constants, including the EEG sample rate and µV scaling, follow open-source implementations and must be checked against GE's computer interface specification for your monitor and software version. Logged recordings are patient data: get the appropriate ethical approval and consent, and de-identify before sharing.

How the figures were made

Every trace, spectrum and DSA on this site was produced by tools/make_figures.py. It is synthetic, so no patient data is involved and every image can be shared freely.

  • Each rhythm is band-limited noise: white noise passed through a gentle band-pass filter and slowly amplitude-modulated, so it waxes and wanes like real EEG rather than looking like a pure sine wave. A 1/f background sits underneath.
  • Each "state" (awake, propofol, sevoflurane ≥ 1 MAC, ketamine and so on) mixes rhythms in the proportions described in the cited literature[1,2].
  • The analysis is the same as the companion script (Welch, Hann, 2 s segments, SEF95 over 0.5–45 Hz). The colour scheme is the script's light theme, and every DSA uses the same fixed −20 to +20 dB scale so drugs can be compared directly.
  • Band-shaded figures are generated twice, once for each band scheme, which is what the switch at the top of the page swaps between.
Limits of synthetic EEG

These are idealised cartoons. Real recordings are noisier and vary more between patients, and drug combinations blur the signatures. Use the clinical examples linked here to see real EEG.

To regenerate the figures: pip install numpy scipy matplotlib, then python tools/make_figures.py. To rebuild the pages after editing content: python build.py (see contributing).
